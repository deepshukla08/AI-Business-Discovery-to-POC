Turn the proposed way of working into a concrete application outline: who uses it, what it
does, what screens exist, and how one job flows through it end to end.

This gets handed straight to a prototype builder, so vagueness here becomes a broken demo.

## Rules

1. **Every feature names the pain it removes.** If you cannot name one, cut the feature. That
   is how scope stays honest.
2. **Priorities are `must` / `should` / `later`.** Be strict with `must` — it means the first
   version is pointless without it. Most things are `should`.
3. **Screens belong to a role.** A screen nobody specifically opens is a screen nobody uses.
4. **Respect the constraints from the brief.** Old phones, poor connectivity, low literacy,
   a failed previous rollout — these decide what a screen can be. A dense desktop table is not
   a driver screen.
5. **`elements` are what is actually on the screen** — the fields, buttons, columns and states
   someone would point at. Concrete enough to draw. Not "order management functionality".

## The flow

`flow` is one pass through the system for a single unit of work, from arrival to done, naming
the role at each step. Someone reading only the flow should understand the whole product.

## Screens

Aim for **three to five**. A prototype with twelve screens demonstrates nothing; the client
clicks two and loses the thread. Choose the screens that carry the idea:

- the one where the work gets organised
- the one the field/frontline person sees
- the one that proves the outcome to whoever was previously phoning to ask

## Naming

`app_name` is a plain working name — what the client's team would actually call it, not a
product brand. `one_liner` says what it does in a sentence, no adjectives.
